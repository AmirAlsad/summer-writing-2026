Alex Hormozi has this idea that you should go accomplish something before you make content about it. You have to have something to show before you can show it. I know it's not the only way he says to make content, but it's one of the big ones, and because of that I've been reflecting on it.

I want to build a personal brand, but only for business. There's no personal motive behind it for me. As do many others that build personal brands. I think Hormozi himself says that, comfort-wise, he would've preferred to stay private. If anything, I'd like to avoid it. I just can't ignore that it'll help with future business growth and opportunities, and because of that I'm willing to put in the work to make it work.

On the note of creating a personal brand, the biggest thing I've got going for me is that I'm an MIT student building a startup. Unfortunately, none of my personal attributes actually feed into what I'm building, so I can't justify the decision through the startup. In other words, my personal brand probably won't work as a lead machine in the short-term. And so, in pursuing this endeavor, I'd be leaning on the idea that my brand will come in handy down the line.

That clears something up. This isn't urgent. A personal brand is an appreciating asset (the whole reason Hormozi pushes content in the first place), which makes it an investment, not an emergency. I don't need to prioritize it like crazy. I need to start on it as a habit, the same way I should be hitting the gym consistently or reading consistently. It's more of a thing that widens what's available to me later, whether that's investors, collaborators, or whatever I build after this.

The point of content, as far as I can tell, is to educate, motivate, or entertain. I want the first two, no doubt, because those actions seem to be how you make an impact in this world. Entertainment is a lesser priority, and, in all honesty, it's the one I'd like to avoid since it's the piece of social media that irks me.

The catch is that even if all you want to do is educate and motivate, you still have to play for the audience's attention, and playing for attention requires doing certain things. But I think there's a fine line to distinguish attention grabbing from entertainment. There's entertainment as the whole point (the reel that exists only to be watched, where the "value" is just an excuse) and there's entertainment as the craft that earns you a hearing in the first place (a good story, a hook, some humor so someone actually stays). The first is the "evil." I put it in quotation marks because I haven't exactly laid out why I think it's "evil", but you can probably see it through my earlier pieces. The second is just teaching. Every good teacher I've had did the second one.

As a side note, I think the news, which would normally fall under the "educate" categorization, belongs in the same bucket as "entertain". I think it's actually more "evil" than typical entertainment since it appears otherwise. I'll probably explore this more in another piece.

> I don't want to be a dopamine merchant.

I'm really glad that I've been writing this summer. It's setting me up towards building a personal brand that actually sits right within my conscience. You can't turn a long, inductive essay into slop. The form won't let you. It demands a certain level of focus to deliver anything. So, the medium ends up doing some of the moral work for me. In other words, I don't have to police my own intentions every second.

Now, there seems to be one last mental barrier. Am I doing harm just by putting my stuff on a platform I think people shouldn't be on? I don't think people should be on Instagram or Facebook. I think they'd learn better from books, which demand focus and don't hand you a hundred other influences on the side. But my content would live on those platforms. And even if someone came for the good thing, they'd hit all the funny, engaging stuff on the way. They'd still end up consuming goyslop. So, am I not just feeding the machine?

I'd rather be an honest man than a rich one. Alhamdulillah, that's what's more favorable in the eyes of Allah (SWT). The question becomes "can I do this honestly?" If yes, go, and the growth is allowed to be whatever it turns out to be.

The first thought is that my staying off the platform doesn't shrink it. To a company like Meta, I'm an insignificant contribution. Me abstaining doesn't cost them much. If anything, it would just remove one of the few "good" things sitting there. With that the decision becomes "reach the person or don't." Almost all the weight is on reaching the person, and almost none on propping up the platform, because I can't prop it up or starve it either way. The second thought is that I don't have to preach that others should "get off the platform." I don't have to say it. A link to a long-form essay already says it. The form is effectively an invitation to go somewhere quieter. An exit off the busy highway.

Giving quick value is fine, and probably necessary. I'd basically just take a piece of the larger essay and point to the rest. But I must be careful when it comes to the execution. Done one way, I post something complete. A real self-contained idea that stands on its own and happens to point at the fuller reasoning for anyone who wants it. Done another way, I end up posting a teaser. Something cut off on purpose so the value only shows up after the click. The first is a gift. The second is a dishonest deal in which I presumably get people's attention and they get nothing in return.

There's an authentic narration from the Prophet Muhammad (SAW) that goes: “Whoever among you sees evil, let him change it with his hand. If he cannot do so, then with his tongue. If he cannot do so, then with his heart, which is the weakest level of faith.” I recently heard this narration and it's given me more peace with where my ideas lead. By putting out beneficial content, at least what I think is beneficial, then I'd be, at least through my intentions, changing the experience that people have on these platforms for the better. And even if I fail in this regard, then I'd still be speaking out against the evil.

Until next time, ✌️.
