Saying "no", to others and to yourself, is quite an essential skill for navigating this world. It stipulates that, if something is not truly the object of focus, then it should be refused on the basis that it's a distraction from what truly matters. Such a stipulation makes it almost impossible to apply the skill without one being wholly convicted in their understanding of what truly matters.

Reflecting on the decisions that I've made in my lifetime (which, to be fair, hasn't been that long of a life compared to other humans), the most regretful moments are those in which I failed to say "no" to what didn't matter and the most fruitful moments are those in which I said "yes" to that which truly matters.

Tying back to the last piece, one of the issues with making the wrong decisions (beyond it being the wrong decision) is that it can compound in commitment. Failing to say "no" at one critical moment can turn that matter into something that **seems to truly matter in the moment but doesn't truly matter in the long run.** After that failure, everything related to that commitment seems like it should be a "yes" until you snap out of the delusion.

Be serious in your focus. There's too many distractions in this world. A reminder for myself and for you.
