The World Cup is here, and the hype around it is like nothing I've ever seen before.

I keep thinking: is society growing more deranged, or am I just getting older now? Deranged is probably too harsh a word. But what I'm trying to point at is the sense that society focuses too much on small, meaningless matters, and that the focus seems to be getting heavier. When I reflect on the World Cup in 2022, things weren't as they are today. Maybe it's the games being more local this time around (i.e. hosted in the U.S.), but I'm not sure that fully explains the feeling.

As I get older, I find myself more and more cynical about the society that we live in. And not just cynical about people (cynicism about people seems natural enough), but about the general society that people construct. The issue is that I genuinely can't tell whether this is a perspective problem or whether reality just happens to correlate with my aging. Everything in my own life is changing, so of course the whole world looks like it's shifting under me.

Now, you could try to settle this objectively, the way a lot of people do, by looking at the raw numbers. How many people are alive, and what their lives are like. By that measure, things are clearly up. Most Americans, if not all, live with more luxury than emperors ever did. And to be clear, while this shows we're "progressing" in the sense that lives are being made easier, this doesn't absolve us from the issue of society being preoccupied with trivial matters. One can be "making progress", which I might add that "making progress" is a remarkably vague descriptor of changes in society because the vision we're progressing towards isn't entirely clear, and still be in a terrible state with respect to other regards. You can make life easier and more comfortable every single year while also having everyone's attention scattered across nothing. I wonder if that's because people having their attention scattered is part of the progress that we're supposedly making.

Thinking about it a bit more, I realized this was never really about the World Cup. It's the same thing I felt with the NBA Finals, and it's the same thing I feel watching trends move across social media. It's not about *what* the attention lands on. It's about *how fast it moves.*

Let me be clear, because I'm not just trying to be a killjoy or party pooper. It's not that I don't like soccer. I love soccer. I'd say I'm more in tune with it than the average person, and I play it whenever I get the chance. What gets to me is watching people who don't like soccer suddenly so invested in these games. You can tell the interest isn't really coming from the sport. It's coming from everything around it. Rooting for the home country or the underdog the way people do in any tournament. Making jokes and references about certain teams or players. Or even just using the games as an excuse to sit around with friends and family. That's all fine on its own. People's interest in almost everything starts socially. But this time, it happened for everyone almost immediately. So much so that I've thought of it as obnoxious, subconsciously, as an extension of my impatience.

For a while, I couldn't figure out why this ties back to social media (for which I had some intuition), but I think I've got it now. Think about what it used to take for a whole society to swing its attention onto one thing. The layer that coordinated all of us was slow (if not non-existent). Newspapers. Scheduled broadcasts. And it had a kind of inertia built into it, because the people running it were selling you the publication, not the next click. The feed that every person receives today on their personal devices broke that in two ways:
1. It dropped the cost of redirecting everyone's attention down to basically nothing.
2. It stopped rewarding you for lingering on anything at all.

It's built to win the next interaction, not your depth on this one, and in that contest, a fresh piece of stimulation tends to beat the one you've already seen. So, the machine keeps manufacturing the current thing and then, by its own logic, throws it away. The bandwagon assembled overnight because joining "the current thing" is frictionless. It also makes it such that it's friction-full to avoid "the current thing."

What's really bothering me, I think, is how quickly we move from one thing to the next, and how that speed seems to keep increasing. NBA Finals, then the World Cup, then whatever trend comes after, then the next political story. Remember the Epstein files? We were all riled up, and then we just... moved on. We never actually resolved anything there. There's a sincere danger in constantly switching focus: we can't linger on anything long enough to actually do anything about it.

Perhaps the issue is just me. It's plausible that the idea of constantly changing focus is just a reflection of my own behavior. Perhaps there are people that truly can focus on important issues. People that won't calm down until the matter is resolved. If that were the case, I suspect we would see some positive change in the world, but I, unfortunately, don't see any. I'd love to hear from anyone who does, because it'd give me some more hope...

Until next time, ✌️.
