The other day, my kitchen sink was clogged. My parents tried fixing it with their usual methods, but they didn't work out. I decided to help them out. We were able to isolate the issue, but the available tools weren't sufficient for fixing it. My mom then started a quick chat with Google Gemini and shared an image of our sink's piping. It's one of those two-sink layouts (and both were clogged), so Gemini gave us the idea of blocking one sink and turning on the garbage disposal to create a pressure difference that would push the water against whatever was clogging the pipes. It worked. The pipes were unclogged and the problem was solved. It was a nice little bonding activity with my parents.

Afterwards, I got to thinking, why didn't I learn about this stuff in school? I managed to learn the physics behind it, and that's with me being proactive about my education. The average person learns neither the physics nor the practical bits. There ought to be a model sink in schools so students can learn all about them. Further, there ought to be a whole class that just revolves around standard plumbing practices that everyone should know. Another class that's all about electricity and wiring. Another class on taxes. It's ridiculous that we expect students to grow up and become thriving members of society when we fail to teach them about the broader systems that society operates in.

It annoys me. These things are so obvious (in my humble opinion), and yet it feels like things will never change. Hearing that from anyone else would prompt me to tell them that they got to be the ones that advocate for change, and I hope to be one that lives up to that.
