People don't pay attention to their decisions enough. And it's not unnatural. I don't think so at least. When I started thinking about that, my mind went to the question of why that is. You see, decisions are just actionable extensions of thought. They're conclusive and have some potential of changing behavior (deciding to quit drugs, for example). Most of our decisions turn out to be subconsciously guided. Imagine you're eating dinner and you had to consciously think about every time you pick up the fork, put it to your mouth, chew, swallow, etc. Sounds pretty mentally exhausting to me. But, the subconscious decisions of ours are influenced by the conscious ones (same example of quitting drugs) And so, when I say "people don't pay attention to their decisions enough," I'm referring to the conscious ones.

I think people can generally reason towards "achieving success" given some proper set of values. But I think where people fall short is that they don't have the drive to think consciously. To ask questions and seek answers. With the lack of conscious decisions, they're subconscious decisions are all fueled by subconscious desires (rather than the conscious desires that are established from what someone values). It's important to distinguish between the values that are ingrained within one's self and those that are chosen by individuals from within themselves. It's never truly one or the other, so it's probably some sort of spectrum. That's beyond the point thought, so I'll digress.

Some people realize that they don't enjoy the "subconscious desires" lifestyle that they've set up. Some never do.

I've found myself more concerned with the decisions that I make than others my age (compared to the vast sea of people). And in no way am I trying to single myself out as special. I seek refuge in Allah (SWT) from thinking too highly of myself. That concern has tended to have me thinking "older" than I am, concerning myself with issues and situations that are "beyond my paygrade" so to speak.

If I had to think about where this concern originated, I think I'd tie it back to the games that I used to play. These games tended to be very tactical. Games like Chess, like Risk (the board game). Technical matters though, contrastingly, were never my focus. By technical matters, I'm referring to anything that relates to execution of a pre-determined plan. In a game like Chess, there is no technical capacity. You just move the piece. In an FPS shooter though, even if you outwit your opponents, you still need to win the gunfights. Getting back to the point, these games certainly contributed to my attention to decisions. They directly reinforced good decisions and punished bad ones, and the good decisions tended to be those that you actually thought through.

Now, if I had to take it another step back and associate my intrigue with these games to something more innate, I would say it was my competitiveness, which seems to just be a fancy way of saying that I sought out attention from others. And the best way to get this attention was to be the indisputable best. That's just speculation though.
